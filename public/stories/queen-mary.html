The Queen Mary: The Haunted Ocean Liner and Stateroom B340

14 min read ☠☠☠☠☠ Coordinates: 33.7527° N, 118.1903° W
🚢

She was the most luxurious ship in the world. When the RMS Queen Mary made her maiden voyage in 1936, she was a floating Art Deco palace — 80,000 square feet of salons, 200 first-class staterooms, the pride of the British merchant fleet. Winston Churchill crossed the Atlantic on her. Royalty sailed on her. For a brief, glamorous window, she was the most beautiful way to cross an ocean that had ever existed.

Then came the war. And then came the deaths. And now the Queen Mary sits permanently docked in Long Beach, California — a hotel, a museum, and by the accounts of thousands of guests and the ship's own management, one of the most haunted places in America.

The Grey Ghost

When World War II broke out, the Queen Mary was stripped of her luxury and pressed into military service. Painted grey for camouflage — earning her the nickname "the Grey Ghost" — she was converted into a troopship capable of carrying enormous numbers of soldiers across the Atlantic, packed far beyond any reasonable capacity. On a single voyage she once carried over 16,000 people, a record that still stands.

The most infamous wartime tragedy did not happen aboard the Queen Mary but was caused by her. In October 1942, while zigzagging at high speed to evade U-boats, the Queen Mary collided with her escort ship, the HMS Curacoa. The much smaller Curacoa was cut clean in half. Under strict wartime orders not to stop — stopping would make her a target for submarines — the Queen Mary sailed on, leaving the men in the water. Over 300 sailors from the Curacoa died. The Queen Mary did not slow down. She could not. The men watched her disappear.

Door 13 and the Engine Room

The engine room is one of the most reported areas of the ship, and the focus is a specific watertight door: Door 13. In July 1966, a young crewman — accounts give his age as 17 or 18, his name usually given as John Pedder — was crushed to death by watertight Door 13 during a drill. He had tried to slip through as it closed.

Since then, the area around Door 13 has been one of the ship's most active locations. Visitors and staff report the apparition of a young man in overalls near the door. Knocking sounds come from the door. People report a feeling of being watched, and some describe a young man who appears briefly and then is simply gone. Ghost tours make Door 13 a centerpiece, and the reports from that specific spot have remained consistent across decades of independent witnesses.

The Pool That's Been Empty for Decades

The first-class swimming pool is, by many accounts, the single most haunted area on the Queen Mary — which is strange, because the pool has been drained and closed for decades. No one swims there. And yet it is where the most consistent and eerie phenomena are reported.

Jackie: A little girl named Jackie is the pool's most famous spirit. Visitors and paranormal investigators report hearing a child's voice near the empty pool — laughing, singing, sometimes answering questions on recording equipment. Jackie is said to have drowned aboard the ship. Her voice has been captured on so many recordings that she is one of the most documented child spirits in American paranormal investigation.

The Woman in the Swimsuit: A woman in a 1930s-style bathing suit is reported near the pool and the changing rooms. She appears, moves as if about to enter the water, and vanishes.

Wet Footprints: The detail that unsettles visitors most — wet footprints appearing on the deck beside a pool that has held no water for decades. There is no source for the water. The footprints appear, and then they dry, and then sometimes they appear again.

Stateroom B340

And then there is B340 — the room the Queen Mary itself closed. For years, guests could not book Stateroom B340, because the reports coming out of it were too consistent and too disturbing. Guests fled in the middle of the night. Lights and faucets turned themselves on and off. Bedcovers were pulled from sleeping guests. The disturbances were frequent enough, and dramatic enough, that management simply took the room out of service.

Then, in a decision that tells you how thoroughly the Queen Mary has embraced its reputation, they reopened B340 — not as a normal room, but explicitly as a paranormal experience. The room now has no television and no clock. What it has instead is paranormal investigation equipment: a spirit board, EMF meters, and other tools for guests who book the room specifically hoping to encounter whatever it is that made B340 uninhabitable in the first place. It sells out constantly. People pay to sleep in the room the ship itself once gave up on.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is the Queen Mary really haunted?
The Queen Mary in Long Beach is considered one of the most haunted locations in America, with the ship's management maintaining paranormal records since the 1960s. At least 49 people died aboard. Reported phenomena include apparitions in the first-class pool area, activity near Door 13 in the engine room, and disturbances in Stateroom B340.
What happened in Stateroom B340?
Stateroom B340 is the Queen Mary's most haunted room. It was closed to guests for years after repeated reports of guests fleeing in the night, lights and faucets turning on by themselves, and bedcovers being pulled off sleepers. It has since reopened as a deliberate paranormal experience, equipped with investigation tools instead of a TV or clock.
Who is the ghost in the Queen Mary pool?
The first-class pool, drained for decades, is one of the most active areas. Visitors report a young girl named Jackie, believed to have drowned aboard, whose voice has been captured on many recordings, and a woman in a vintage swimsuit near the changing rooms. Wet footprints reportedly appear beside the empty pool.
How do you visit the Queen Mary?
The Queen Mary is docked at 1126 Queens Highway, Long Beach, California, and operates as a hotel. Coordinates: 33.7527° N, 118.1903° W. Guests can stay overnight in original staterooms including B340. Haunted Encounters ghost tours are available and often sell out, especially near Halloween.
