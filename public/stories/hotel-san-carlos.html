Hotel San Carlos: The Ghost of Leone Jensen in Phoenix

9 min read SSS Coordinates: 33.4484 N, 112.0740 W
🏨

In the heart of downtown Phoenix stands an elegant reminder of a more glamorous age: the Hotel San Carlos, a 1928 landmark that once hosted Hollywood stars in the golden years of the desert city. With its Italian Renaissance styling and vintage charm, it is a beautiful piece of Arizona history. But the San Carlos carries more than old-world elegance. It is widely considered the most haunted hotel in Phoenix, home to the tragic ghost of a young woman named Leone Jensen, and to the laughter of children whose story reaches back even further than the hotel itself.

The haunting of the San Carlos is layered, drawing on both the tragedy that struck soon after it opened and the history of what stood on the land long before.

The Well and the Schoolhouse

Before the Hotel San Carlos rose on the corner, the site was home to Phoenix's first schoolhouse, built in the 1870s. On the property was a well, a vital water source in the desert. When the grand hotel was constructed in 1928, it was built over this historic ground, incorporating the old well beneath it. That connection to the early school is said to explain one strand of the hotel's haunting: the spirits of children.

Guests and staff over the decades have reported the sounds of children laughing and running through the halls, and the feeling of small, unseen presences, phenomena often attributed to the young pupils of the long-vanished schoolhouse, still at play on the ground where their school once stood.

Leone Jensen

The hotel's most famous ghost arrived with tragedy, not long after the San Carlos opened. Leone Jensen was a young woman who, according to the hotel's history, took her own life by leaping from the roof of the building in 1928. Her death cast a shadow over the glamorous new hotel, and her spirit is said to have remained ever since.

Over the decades, guests and staff have reported seeing a woman in white drifting through the upper floors and hallways, believed to be Leone. She is described as a sorrowful, gentle presence rather than a frightening one, a young woman still lingering in the hotel where her life ended. Her story is the heart of the San Carlos's haunted reputation, a reminder of how often beauty and tragedy are entwined in historic places.

A Haunted Landmark

Beyond Leone and the children, the San Carlos has accumulated the familiar signs of a haunted hotel: cold spots, flickering lights, objects that move on their own, and the sense of unseen company in quiet corridors. Its combination of a tragic suicide, the spirits of schoolchildren, and the old well beneath the building gives it an unusually rich haunted history for a working downtown hotel.

Rather than hide from this reputation, the Hotel San Carlos embraces it. Its history as a glamorous celebrity destination and its ghost stories together form part of its identity and its appeal, drawing guests who come as much for the legends as for the vintage elegance.

Staying at the San Carlos

Today the Hotel San Carlos operates as a boutique historic hotel in downtown Phoenix, welcoming guests who want a taste of 1920s glamour, and perhaps an encounter with its resident spirits. Visitors can book rooms, including on the floors tied to its ghost stories, and walk the same halls where a woman in white is said to drift and children's laughter is said to echo.

The Hotel San Carlos stands as a graceful survivor of old Phoenix, a place where the jazz-age elegance of the desert city lives on, shadowed gently by the ghost of Leone Jensen and the playful spirits of a schoolhouse that vanished long ago. It is a haunting of sorrow and nostalgia rather than terror, fitting for a hotel that has watched nearly a century of the city pass by.
