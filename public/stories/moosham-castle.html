Moosham Castle: Austria's Witch Castle and the Trials of Death

11 min read SSSS Coordinates: 47.1011 N, 13.6469 E
🏰

High in the Lungau region of Salzburg, Austria, surrounded by alpine forest, stands a castle with a name the locals say in a lower voice: Schloss Moosham, the Witch Castle. It is a beautiful medieval fortress, but its beauty sits on top of one of the darkest chapters of European history, a chapter of torture, mass execution, and fear. Hundreds of people are believed to have been condemned to death here during the witch hunts, and in the centuries since, Moosham has gathered a second reputation: as one of the most haunted castles in Austria, where the suffering of the accused is said to linger in the stone.

Moosham dates back to the medieval period and served as a seat of administration and justice for the region. It was that role, as a place of justice, that turned it into a place of horror.

The Witch Trials

In the late 17th century, Salzburg was gripped by a wave of witch hunting, most infamously the Zauberer-Jackl trials, named after a figure called "Sorcerer Jackl" who was never caught. In the search for him and his supposed followers, a terrible machinery of persecution ground into motion. Many of the accused were society's most vulnerable, poor people, beggars, the homeless, and children, swept up on suspicion of witchcraft and sorcery.

Moosham Castle served as a court and place of imprisonment where the accused were interrogated, tortured into confessions, and sentenced. Hundreds of people are believed to have been executed in connection with these trials. The castle's lower chambers, used for holding and questioning prisoners, became rooms where unimaginable pain was inflicted in the name of law and religion.

The Werewolf Legend

Moosham's dark reputation did not end with the witch trials. In the 19th century, the area around the castle was troubled by a disturbing series of incidents: deer and cattle found dead and mutilated. In a time still steeped in superstition, blame fell on people associated with the castle, who were accused of being werewolves responsible for the killings. This later werewolf legend layered a second supernatural dread over Moosham, cementing its place in the folklore of the cursed and the monstrous.

Whether the mutilations were the work of wolves, poachers, or something else, the legend stuck, and Moosham became a castle associated not only with witches but with shape-shifters, a double darkness few places carry.

First the witch trials, where hundreds were tortured and executed. Then, centuries later, mutilated animals in the forest and whispers of werewolves living in the castle. Moosham carries two kinds of dread at once.

The Haunting

Given its history, it is little surprise that Moosham is considered deeply haunted. Visitors and staff report the familiar vocabulary of the haunted castle, footsteps in empty halls, doors that move on their own, sudden drops in temperature, and the feeling of being watched, but at Moosham these are tied to an unusually concrete and documented history of suffering. The torture chambers and holding cells are described as especially oppressive, heavy with a sorrow and dread that visitors say they can feel physically.

Some report the sense of a presence following them through the lower rooms, or a sudden heaviness and difficulty breathing in the spaces where prisoners were once held. Unlike hauntings built on vague legend, Moosham's reported activity is anchored to real, recorded atrocity, which gives it a particular weight.

Visiting Moosham

Today Moosham Castle is preserved as a historic site and museum, open to visitors who can tour its rooms, including the chambers tied to the witch trials. Guides tell the castle's true history, the trials, the torture, the executions, alongside its ghostly reputation. It is a place that does not shy away from its past, presenting the human cruelty of the witch hunts as the real horror beneath the ghost stories.

Moosham is a reminder that the most frightening haunted places are often those where the worst things genuinely happened. Whatever visitors feel in its torture rooms, the history is undeniable: hundreds of ordinary, frightened people were destroyed here on charges of witchcraft. The Witch Castle earned its name not through legend, but through blood.

Frequently Asked

Why is Moosham Castle called the Witch Castle?
Because it was the site of mass witch trials in the late 17th century. Hundreds of people, many accused in the Zauberer-Jackl trials, were interrogated, tortured, and executed there. The scale of suffering gave it the nickname Witch Castle and its haunted reputation.
What were the Salzburg witch trials?
A wave of witch hunting including the Zauberer-Jackl trials of the 1670s-80s, in which many poor, homeless, or young people were accused of witchcraft, tortured into confessions, and executed. Moosham served as a court where interrogations and sentencing took place.
Is Moosham Castle connected to werewolves?
Yes, in local legend. In the 19th century, mutilated deer and cattle were found near the castle, and people associated with it were accused of being werewolves. This werewolf legend added another dark layer on top of the witch-trial history.
Can you visit Moosham Castle?
Yes. It is in the Lungau region of Salzburg, Austria, open as a historic site and museum. Coordinates: 47.1011 N, 13.6469 E. Guided tours cover its history, including the witch trials and torture chambers. It is popular with those interested in dark history and the paranormal.
