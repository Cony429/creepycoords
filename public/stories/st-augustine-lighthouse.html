St. Augustine Lighthouse: The Drowned Sisters and the Tower Ghosts

11 min read SSSS Coordinates: 29.8853 N, 81.2886 W
🏭

A lighthouse is meant to be a place of safety, a light that keeps sailors off the rocks. The St. Augustine Lighthouse, its black-and-white spiral rising 165 feet over Florida's oldest city, has saved countless ships. But it is also a place of loss, and the losses came before the light was even finished. Two little girls drowned in the bay during its construction, and their laughter, people say, still drifts up the 219 iron steps of the tower. It is one of the most famously haunted lighthouses in America, and the sorrow at its heart is that of children.

The current tower was completed in 1874, replacing an older Spanish-era structure that had fallen to coastal erosion. During its construction, tragedy struck the family overseeing the work.

The Drowned Sisters

The superintendent of construction had brought his family to live near the site, and his daughters played among the building materials as the tower rose. Supplies were moved from the water up to the site on a small rail cart that ran down to the bay. The children loved to ride it. One day the cart, with several of the girls aboard, rolled down the tracks and into the water. Two of the sisters drowned, young girls whose deaths cast a permanent shadow over the lighthouse before its lamp was ever lit.

They are the spirits most often reported at the tower. Visitors describe children's laughter echoing in the stairwell, small giggles with no source, and the fleeting glimpse of a girl in a blue dress. Of all the phenomena reported at St. Augustine, the presence of the sisters is the most consistent, and the most poignant.

The Man in the Tower

The children are not alone. Visitors and staff have long reported the figure of a man in the tower and the old keeper's workshop, believed to be a former lighthouse keeper who remains at his post. He is described appearing on the stairs and at the top of the tower, sometimes as a full figure, sometimes as a shadow that moves against the curve of the wall and vanishes.

One keeper is said to have fallen to his death while working on the tower, adding another layer to its history of loss. The combination of drowned children and a devoted keeper gives the lighthouse two very different hauntings occupying the same narrow spiral of iron and brick.

Climb the 219 steps in the quiet and you may hear it: the light laughter of little girls somewhere above or below you on the spiral stairs, always just out of sight around the curve.

Shadows on the Stairs

Beyond the sisters and the keeper, the lighthouse has produced a long catalogue of reports: footsteps climbing and descending the empty staircase, shadow figures at the tower's summit, cold spots, and voices captured on audio recordings by investigators. The site's own staff have shared experiences, and the lighthouse has been the subject of numerous paranormal investigations and television features, which helped make it nationally known.

The keepers' house at the base has its own reports, including sounds of movement and the sense of a presence in rooms that should be empty. For a working historic site, St. Augustine is unusually open about its ghosts, embracing the stories rather than hiding them.

Visiting the Light

Today the St. Augustine Lighthouse and Maritime Museum welcomes visitors to climb the tower by day and to join after-dark paranormal tours by night. From the top, the view over the oldest city in the United States is stunning. But many who make the climb are listening as much as looking, waiting for the small sounds that have made this beautiful tower one of America's most haunted, the laughter of two sisters who never grew up, still playing on the stairs of the lighthouse their father helped build.

Frequently Asked

Why is the St. Augustine Lighthouse haunted?
It is tied to the deaths of two young sisters who drowned during construction in the 1870s when a rail cart they were riding rolled into the bay. Their deaths, along with those of keepers and workers, are the foundation of its reputation as one of America's most haunted lighthouses.
Who are the ghost girls at the lighthouse?
The two young sisters who drowned during construction, often associated with the Pittee family. Visitors report children's laughter, giggles on the stairs, and a girl in a blue dress. It is the most commonly reported phenomenon at the tower.
What do people experience there?
Children's laughter and footsteps on the spiral staircase, the apparition of a man believed to be a former keeper, shadow figures, and voices on recordings. The site has been investigated by many paranormal teams and featured on television.
Can you visit the St. Augustine Lighthouse?
Yes. The St. Augustine Lighthouse and Maritime Museum in Florida is open to the public, and you can climb the 219 steps. Coordinates: 29.8853 N, 81.2886 W. It offers daytime visits and popular after-dark paranormal tours, especially around Halloween.
