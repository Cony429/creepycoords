Mary King's Close: Edinburgh's Buried Street and Little Annie

10 min read SSSS Coordinates: 55.9497 N, 3.1903 W
🏗

Beneath the bustling Royal Mile in Edinburgh, hidden under the city's municipal buildings, lies a secret the modern street above has nearly forgotten: a whole network of old lanes and houses, frozen in time and sealed in darkness. This is Mary King's Close, once a crowded, living part of Edinburgh's old town, now a buried warren of stone streets beneath the city. It is a place soaked in the history of plague, poverty, and death, and it is home to one of the most beloved and heartbreaking ghosts in all of Scotland: a little girl named Annie.

To understand Mary King's Close, you have to understand how Edinburgh's old town was built: upward and crammed together, tenements stacked many stories high along narrow alleys called closes, teeming with people living almost on top of one another in conditions that were often filthy and disease-ridden.

The Street That Was Buried

Mary King's Close was one of these narrow lanes, named after a woman who owned property there in the 17th century. It was a busy, ordinary place of merchants and families. But in the 18th century, as the city reorganized, a large municipal building was constructed over part of the old town, and the lower sections of the close were sealed off and built upon. The street did not vanish, it was entombed, its lower floors and alleys preserved in darkness beneath the new construction.

For centuries the buried close sat largely forgotten beneath the city, accumulating legends. In time it was rediscovered and opened to the public, and visitors found an eerie, remarkably preserved slice of old Edinburgh, complete with the rooms where ordinary people once lived and died.

Plague and Darkness

Edinburgh was struck repeatedly by plague, and Mary King's Close is closely tied to that terror in popular memory. The most dramatic legend claims that plague victims were sealed inside the close and left to die, walled up with the disease. Historians regard the harshest versions of this story as exaggeration, the reality of quarantine was grim but more complex, but what is beyond doubt is that this was a cramped, poor, unsanitary place where sickness spread easily and death was a constant neighbor.

That heavy history, generations of hard lives and hard deaths packed into these dark lanes, gives the close its oppressive, haunted atmosphere. Visitors often describe a weight to the air, a sense of sorrow and unease in the sealed stone rooms where the light of day has not reached for centuries.

A psychic sensed a little girl crying, not from fear of the dark or the dead, but because she had lost her doll. Visitors have left toys for Annie ever since, piling her room with gifts for a child who never grew up.

Little Annie

The most famous presence in Mary King's Close is the ghost of a young girl called Annie. The legend took shape when a Japanese psychic visiting the close reportedly encountered the spirit of a small, sad girl in one of the rooms. The psychic sensed that Annie's sorrow came not from the plague or the darkness, but from something achingly childlike: she had lost her doll.

Moved by the story, the psychic bought a doll and left it for Annie. Word spread, and ever since, visitors have brought toys, dolls, coins, and small gifts, leaving them in Annie's room until it overflows with offerings for the little ghost. It has become one of the most touching sights in any haunted place anywhere: a dark, cold room deep beneath a city, piled with bright toys for a child who died long ago, so that she might finally have something to play with.

Visiting the Close

Today The Real Mary King's Close is open as a guided tour attraction beneath the Royal Mile. Costumed guides lead visitors down into the preserved streets and dwellings, telling the true history of the close, its plague, its people, and its poverty, alongside its ghost stories. Walking the buried lanes, stepping into rooms where families once lived their whole lives, is a genuinely atmospheric and often moving experience.

Mary King's Close endures because it combines real, documented history with one of the gentlest and saddest hauntings imaginable. It is not a place of violent poltergeists or screaming apparitions. Its power lies in the quiet dark of a buried street, and in the pile of toys left for a little girl named Annie, a small, kind gesture from the living to the forgotten dead of old Edinburgh.

Frequently Asked

What is Mary King's Close?
A network of narrow old streets and dwellings, known as closes, buried beneath the Royal Mile in Edinburgh. Once a bustling part of the crowded old city, the closes were partially demolished and built over in the 18th century, sealing parts underground. It is now open as The Real Mary King's Close.
Was it sealed because of plague?
Edinburgh suffered terribly from plague, and the close is strongly associated with it. Legend claims victims were sealed inside and left to die, though historians consider the most dramatic versions exaggerated. It was certainly a cramped, poor, disease-ridden place where many lived and died.
Who is Little Annie?
The most famous ghost of the close, said to be a young girl who died there. A psychic reportedly sensed her sadness over a lost doll. Since then, visitors have left toys, dolls, and gifts for Annie in her room, which has become a touching shrine to the child ghost.
Can you visit Mary King's Close?
Yes. The Real Mary King's Close is open as a guided underground tour beneath the Royal Mile in Edinburgh. Coordinates: 55.9497 N, 3.1903 W. Costumed guides lead groups through the preserved streets, telling its history and ghostly reputation. Booking ahead is recommended.
